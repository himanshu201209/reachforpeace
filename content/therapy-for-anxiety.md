## How do I know if I need therapy for anxiety?

If worry, tension or panic is getting in the way of your sleep, work or relationships, it's worth talking to a psychologist. You don't need a diagnosis, or the right words, to start.

### Overthinking and worry loops

The same thoughts on repeat, endless "what ifs", and trouble letting go of small things.

### Feeling on edge

Restlessness, tension and a constant sense that something is about to go wrong.

### Panicky moments

Sudden waves of fear or a racing heart that leave you shaken and wary of the next one.

### Pulling back from life

Avoiding plans, calls or tasks that used to feel manageable.

## What does therapy for anxiety involve?

When anxiety takes over, even small things can feel like too much. In therapy, you’ll work with the psychologist at your own pace to understand what keeps setting off the worry and find simple ways to feel calmer day to day.

You don’t need to have it all figured out before you start. You’ll work it out together.

### Notice the pattern

Understand what sets off your worry, what keeps it going, and how it shows up in your body and your day.

### Build coping tools

Learn practical strategies you can use in daily life, not just in sessions.

### Ease the need to control everything

Gently loosen habits that bring short-term relief but keep anxiety going.

### Build for the long term

Work towards steadier days you can sustain, not a quick fix.

Sessions are non-judgemental. You set the pace, and nothing is forced.

## Common questions about therapy for anxiety

### Can therapy help with anxiety and overthinking?

Therapy can help many people with anxiety and overthinking. Dr. Lisha Jindal helps you understand your worry patterns and build practical ways to cope, using evidence-based, client-centred methods tailored to you. Results vary from person to person.

### Do I need a diagnosis to start anxiety therapy?

No. You can start with what you’re noticing, whether that’s constant worry, restlessness, overthinking, or difficulty switching off. You don’t need a diagnosis or a label to reach out for therapy.

### Is anxiety therapy available online?

Yes. All sessions with Reach for Peace are online by secure video or audio, by appointment.

### Will I be prescribed medication for anxiety?

No. Dr. Lisha Jindal is a psychologist and does not prescribe medication. If medication might help, we can coordinate with a psychiatrist.

### How many therapy sessions will I need for anxiety?

There's no fixed number; it varies from person to person. Some people come for a few sessions to work on one concern, while others prefer ongoing support. You can discuss this on your fit call and review it as you go.

### What if I feel anxious during an online session?

That's okay, and it's a common worry. You can pause, slow down or switch from video to audio. Everything goes at your pace, without judgement.

### Who do I call if I'm having a panic attack or feel unsafe right now?

Call Tele-MANAS on 14416, India's free government mental-health helpline, available 24×7. If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services. Reach for Peace is not a 24×7 or crisis service.
