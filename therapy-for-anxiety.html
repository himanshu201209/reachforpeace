How do I know if I need therapy for anxiety?

If worry, tension or panicky feelings are getting in the way of your sleep, work or relationships, it's reasonable to talk to a psychologist. You don't need a diagnosis or a perfect explanation to start.

Overthinking and worry loops

The same thoughts on repeat, "what if" scenarios, and trouble letting go of small things.

Feeling on edge

Restlessness, tension and a constant sense that something is about to go wrong.

Panicky moments

Sudden waves of fear or a racing heart that leave you shaken and wary of the next one.

What does therapy for anxiety involve?

In therapy for anxiety, you and Dr. Lisha look at what triggers your worry and what keeps it going, then build practical ways to cope. She uses evidence-based, client-centred methods adapted to you, not a fixed script.

Notice the pattern

Look at what sets off the worry, what keeps it going, and how it shows up in your body and your day.

Build coping tools

Learn practical strategies you can use between sessions, not just during them.

Ease the need to control everything

Gently loosen the habits that bring short-term relief but keep anxiety going.

Aim for long-term wellness

Work towards steadier days you can sustain, rather than a quick fix.

Sessions are a non-judgemental space. You set the pace, and nothing is forced.

Common questions about therapy for anxiety

Can therapy help with anxiety and overthinking?

Therapy can help many people with anxiety and overthinking. It gives you a space to understand your worry patterns and build practical coping strategies. Dr. Lisha Jindal uses evidence-based, client-centred methods tailored to what you're going through. Results vary from person to person.

Do I need a diagnosis before starting anxiety therapy?

No. You can start with what you're noticing, such as constant worry, restlessness or trouble switching off. You don't need a label to book.

Is anxiety therapy available online?

Yes. All sessions with Reach for Peace are online by secure video or audio, by appointment. There is no in-person clinic.

Will I be prescribed medication for anxiety?

No. Dr. Lisha Jindal is a psychologist and does not prescribe medication. If medication might help, we can coordinate with a psychiatrist.

How many therapy sessions will I need for anxiety?

It varies from person to person, so there is no fixed number. Some people come for a few sessions to work on a specific concern, and others prefer ongoing support. You can discuss this on your fit call and review it as you go.

What if I feel anxious during an online session?

That's okay, and it's a common worry. You can pause, slow down or switch from video to audio. Sessions go at your pace in a non-judgemental space.

Who do I call if I'm having a panic attack or feel unsafe right now?

Call Tele-MANAS on 14416, India's free government mental-health helpline, available 24×7. If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services. Reach for Peace is not a 24×7 or crisis service.

Can I do anxiety therapy in Hindi?

Yes. Sessions are available in English and Hindi.
